Why Online Safety Matters

The internet is a powerful tool — we use it for school, games, chatting, and learning. But it's also full of risk. Being smart online keeps you safe in real life too.

01 Hackers targeting weak accounts
02 Online scams disguised as good deals
03 Cyberbullying and harassment

// 02 — Scams

Real Scam Examples

These aren't hypothetical — they happen every day, to real people.

⚠ PHISHING

The Fake Bank Email

Ahmed received an email that looked like it was from his bank, asking him to "verify" his account by clicking a link. He entered his login info — and the next day, his account was emptied.

⚠ ONLINE SHOPPING

The Handbag That Never Came

Sarah found a designer handbag on a site offering huge discounts. She paid $150, but it never arrived. When she tried to contact support, the website was gone.

⚠ FAKE JOB

The $100 "Training Fee"

Lina applied for a remote job online. After a fake interview, they said she got the job — but needed to pay $100 for "training materials." She paid, and never heard from them again.

// 03 — Ethics

The Ethics of Cybersecurity

Security skills are powerful — how you use them defines whether you're protecting people or preying on them.

⚖

White-Hat vs Black-Hat

White-hat hackers find weaknesses to fix them, with permission. Black-hat hackers exploit those same weaknesses to steal, spy, or cause harm. Intent and consent are what separate the two.

🔒

Respect Others' Privacy

Just because you can access someone's messages, photos, or accounts doesn't mean you should. Snooping through someone's data without consent is a violation, technical skill or not.

🚫

Knowledge Isn't a Weapon

Learning how scams and exploits work is for defense, not offense. Using that knowledge to harass, stalk, or extort someone turns a safety skill into a harmful one.

📢

Responsible Disclosure

Found a real security flaw? Report it to the company instead of exploiting it or posting it publicly. Ethical researchers disclose privately and give teams time to fix it.

💬

Digital Empathy

Cyberbullying, leaked photos, and doxxing aren't "just online" — they cause real harm. Treat people online with the same respect you'd owe them face-to-face.

🤝

Build Trust, Don't Break It

The strongest security cultures are built on trust. Every ethical choice you make online reinforces that trust instead of eroding it.

// 05 — Tips

Safety Tips

Five habits that block most attacks before they start.

Aa

Strong, Unique Passwords

Mix upper/lowercase, numbers, and symbols. Never reuse "123456" or your name.

2FA

Two-Factor Authentication

Even a stolen password won't work without your second code.

AV

Antivirus Software

Scans your device, warns of harmful sites, blocks dangerous files.

••

Keep Info Private

Your address, phone number and school name aren't for strangers.

?!

Think Before You Click

Check the URL. If it looks too good to be true, it probably is.
